Running your first powerlifting meet feels bigger than it needs to be. Most
of the difficulty isn't the sport — it's the admin around it: getting
lifters registered, taking payment, assigning flights, and getting accurate
scores up on a board fast enough that nobody's stood around wondering who
won. Here's a step-by-step approach that works whether you're running a
12-lifter club meet or a 60-lifter open.

## 1. Pick your format before you pick your date

Decide early whether you're running a single-platform meet (one flight at a
time, simpler to judge and score, slower overall) or a multi-platform meet
(faster, but needs more equipment and more qualified judges). For a first
meet, single-platform is almost always the right call — you can scale up
once you've run one successfully.

## 2. Set your divisions and weight classes upfront

Decide which federation's weight classes and age divisions you're using
(or run an unaffiliated meet with your own categories) before you open
registration. Changing divisions mid-registration causes real headaches —
lifters pick a division when they sign up, and your scoring depends on it
being correct from day one.

## 3. Open registration with real ticket tiers

Give lifters a clear registration window with a firm close date, and price
it to cover your venue, equipment hire and officials. On Athlo, you set up
your event once — venue or virtual details, competition divisions and entry
price — and lifters register and pay directly through the page you publish.
Entries are capped automatically once you hit capacity, so you're not
chasing a spreadsheet to see if you're oversold.

## 4. Confirm your judging panel and equipment early

You need at least three qualified judges per platform, working weight
plates that match your federation's calibration standard, and a reliable
way to signal lift results (lights or flags). Confirm this at least a month
out — judges are usually volunteers with their own schedules.

## 5. Run your flights and score in real time

On the day, the biggest failure mode is a scoring delay — lifters and
spectators losing track of standings because someone's updating a
spreadsheet between attempts. Whatever tool you use, make sure placements
update live as lifts are confirmed, and that lifters can see where they
stand without asking.

## 6. Get paid without chasing invoices

If you've taken entry fees through a platform with built-in payments, this
step is already done — funds settle automatically once the transaction
clears, minus whatever platform fee applies. If you're still collecting
entry fees by bank transfer or cash on the day, this is usually the part
that eats the most of an organiser's time after the meet is over.
